Four conditions, four pages

Start with the one that sounds most like you.

Neck and shoulder pain

Pain across the neck, into the shoulder, or both, often with a reduced range of motion so you cannot turn or tilt your head as far as you used to. We check whether the problem is muscular or whether a nerve in the neck is involved, because those are treated differently.

Neck and shoulder pain →

Low back pain

Pain low in the back that may stay local or may travel into the hip and leg. We image the area and test it before we treat it, rather than working from where it hurts.

Low back pain →

Headaches and migraines

Some headaches begin in the neck. Those are called cervicogenic headaches, and they are common after a collision or after long stretches held in one position. When the pattern points at the neck, that is where we look.

Headaches and migraines →

Sciatica

Sciatica is irritation of the nerve that runs from your lower back through the buttock and down the leg. It usually shows up on one side as radiating pain, numbness or weakness rather than as back pain alone.

05 / Discs and radiating pain

Disc problems: bulging, herniated and inflamed discs

The discs are the cushions between the bones of your spine. When one bulges, herniates or becomes inflamed, it can press on nearby nerve tissue and send pain, numbness or weakness away from your spine and into an arm or a leg. Disc pain is often worse when you sit, bend or lift, and where you feel it is frequently not where the problem is.

We use on-site digital X-ray to look at the structure and nerve testing to find out whether a nerve is actually involved, then plan from what the tests show. Non-surgical spinal decompression is one of the options. Individual results vary.

The test that settles it

Carpal tunnel syndrome

Carpal tunnel syndrome is compression of the median nerve at the wrist. It shows up as numbness, tingling or weakness in the hand, often worst at night or after repetitive work. Hand symptoms can also come from the neck, so where you feel it does not tell you where it starts.

Nerve conduction velocity testing is what separates the two. It measures how fast a signal travels along the nerve, so it identifies the problem instead of inferring it from your symptoms. We perform it here, on site, in a single visit, with no outside referral.

07 / Also treated here

Sports injuries, scoliosis and stress

  1. Sports injuries

    Strains, sprains and joint injuries from training or competition, including the ones that keep returning to the same spot. We assess the injured tissue and the mechanics around it, because a recurring injury usually has a cause upstream of where it hurts.

    Chiropractic care →

  2. Scoliosis

    Scoliosis is a sideways curvature of the spine. We assess how the curve is loading your back and limiting your movement, and we manage it conservatively. Individual results vary.

    Chiropractic care →

  3. Stress-related tension

    Sustained stress holds the muscles of the neck, jaw and upper back in constant tension, and that tension turns into pain. We treat the physical side of it, and we make no claim about treating the cause of your stress. You will see a figure elsewhere in this market putting stress behind 80-90% of disease. We do not repeat it, because we have not found a source for it.

A different track

If your pain started with a collision

Whiplash is a rapid neck acceleration-deceleration injury. In a collision your head is thrown forward and back faster than your neck can control, and the soft tissue takes the load. Symptoms often surface days later, once inflammation builds, so walking away from the scene feeling fine does not mean you were not injured.

Two things change when a collision is involved. Your injury needs to be measured and written down early, and your care needs to be documented in a form that holds up while a claim is open. That is a different track, and it starts here.
