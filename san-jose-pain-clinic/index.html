02 / What we can measure here

Five instruments, one building, one visit

  • Electromyography (EMG).

    Reads the electrical signal a muscle gives off at rest and under load. A muscle whose nerve supply has been disturbed fires differently, and the tracing shows it.

  • Nerve conduction velocity testing.

    Times an electrical signal travelling along a nerve. Where the signal slows down is where to look.

  • Diagnostic vascular studies.

    Look at blood flow rather than nerve signal, and show where flow is restricted. No needle, no dye.

  • Diagnostic ultrasound.

    Puts a picture to muscle, tendon and ligament, the structures that take the load in a whiplash injury.

  • High-frequency digital X-ray.

    Bone, spinal alignment and joint spacing, and the first thing we look at when a fracture needs ruling out.

The first three are the ones worth naming. In a sweep of 53 published pages across six competing San Jose auto-injury practices, measured 2026-08-20, none of them advertised any of the three. The last two are ordinary and we will not dress them up: four of those six advertise diagnostic ultrasound, and digital X-ray is standard equipment across this market. What any of these studies finds is not the same from one patient to the next.

03 / Were you hit in a car?

Start here. Collision care runs on a different track.

Feeling fine today proves less than it seems. Adrenaline masks pain for a few hours, and injured soft tissue swells on its own schedule, so stiffness, headache and a neck that will not turn tend to arrive on day two or day three. That is a mechanism, not a scare tactic.

  1. Rule out an emergency first. Confusion, a severe or worsening headache, vomiting, any loss of consciousness, or numbness or weakness running into an arm or a leg needs an emergency room, not an appointment.

  2. Get examined and documented. A record written days after the crash carries more weight than the same account given from memory eight months later.

  3. Then decide about an attorney. We are a clinic, not a law office. We do not give legal advice and we will not push you toward hiring anyone.

If you were seen in an emergency room, it did a different job and did it under time pressure. An ER rules out what can kill you, a bleed, a fracture, an organ injury, then discharges you with pain medication. It is not built to be a nerve and soft-tissue work-up, because the studies it runs were looking for something else. That is the gap we close afterwards. What the testing turns up is particular to your collision and to you.

04 / What a measurement changes

Description is disputable. A recorded value is harder to wave away.

Ten out of ten is your judgement of your own pain, and it matters. A conduction velocity is a figure the equipment produced, which can be compared left against right and produced again next month to show change. Both belong in your file. Only one of them is comparable over time.

It matters for the paperwork too. Photographs of a car tend to drive the conversation about a claim, and vehicle damage is a poor predictor of occupant injury. We examine the person instead. All primary testing runs in this building in a single visit, so nothing sits waiting weeks on an outside appointment while the record of your injury is still being built. When a case needs something we do not have here, MRI for example, we say so and refer you on. What a study shows, and what it changes about your treatment, differs case by case.

07 / Who pays while your claim is open

The money question, answered before you have to ask it

There is no charge for the first consultation and evaluation. If you were injured in a collision, care can often go ahead on a lien, which means nothing is paid by you while your claim is open, if you qualify.

A lien is a written arrangement for the clinic to be paid out of the case when it resolves. It has terms, and one of them covers what happens if the case does not resolve at all. We will not tell you that everything is covered, because what is covered depends on your policy and on your case. We read both with you before treatment starts rather than after, and we tell you what we find.
