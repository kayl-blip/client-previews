Accessibility Statement

Last reviewed 10 August 2026. Applies to every page on this site.

This statement describes the site as it is actually built. It is not a conformance claim and it is not an audit result. Where this site falls short, that is written down here too.

This is a small Roseville practice with one practitioner, so this page is written the way we would say it in the office. If you cannot read something here, cannot reach a link with a keyboard, or cannot work out what a page is asking you to do, that is a fault on our side, not yours.

Accessibility on this site is built into the markup rather than bolted on afterwards. There is no overlay widget, no accessibility toolbar and no third-party script that promises to fix the page for you. Those tools sit on top of a page and try to guess what it means. We would rather the page mean the right thing in the first place.

Utility and legal pages like this one sit in the last tier of the site, alongside patient information, the forms page and the sitemap. They are the compliance floor every practice in this market is expected to meet, and they are kept current rather than left to rot.

What is on this page

  • What we have built in

What we have built in

The site this replaced had 37 pages. Across all 37 there was not one main heading, not one canonical link and no practice schema at all, and exactly one page carried its own meta description. The rebuild fixes the heading structure and gives every page its own description. That is the markup level, which is where accessibility actually lives. The canonical links and the practice schema are search-facing rather than accessibility work, and both are now in place on every page of this site.

One real heading structure per page
Every page opens on a single main heading and steps down in order underneath it, so a screen reader can be used to skim the page the way a sighted reader skims it. This page is the example: one main heading, then the section headings you can see in the index above.
Colour contrast measured, not eyeballed
The colour system was checked against the WCAG AA floor of 4.5 to 1 before it was locked, on the grounds the text actually sits on. Body text measures 14.15 to 1 on the page ground and 12.99 to 1 on the tinted band; white text on the dark band measures 14.64 to 1; the smallest accent text clears 4.54 to 1. The old site's light cyan was retired as a text colour for exactly this reason: white on it does not clear AA at body size.
Keyboard reach and a focus ring you can see
Every link, button, form field and menu control can be reached and operated with a keyboard alone, and the control you are currently on is marked with a visible outline rather than a faint colour shift. There is a skip link at the top of every page that jumps straight past the navigation to the main content.
Text alternatives that say something
Every image that carries meaning has alternative text describing what it shows on that particular page. Decorative artwork is marked as decorative so a screen reader passes over it rather than reading a filename aloud.
Targets you can hit
Buttons, filter controls and list links are sized so they can be tapped without precision, which matters on a phone and matters more with a tremor or a splinted hand. The phone number in the header is a real link on every page and at every screen width.
Text that reflows
The layout is built from relative units, so enlarging text in your browser reflows the page instead of clipping it or forcing you to scroll sideways. Nothing on this site depends on a fixed pixel width to stay readable.
Motion you can switch off
If your device is set to reduce motion, the transitions and hover lifts on this site stand still. That setting is honoured, not overridden.

The standard we target

We are working towards WCAG 2.1 Level AA. This site has not been independently audited, and we do not claim conformance with it. Naming a standard we are aiming at is honest; claiming to have met one without an audit to show for it is not.

Accessibility here is built into the markup rather than bolted on, which means you should be able to use this site regardless of how you browse it: with a screen reader, with a keyboard and no mouse, with text scaled up, with motion turned down, or on a phone in one hand. That is the point of doing it this way rather than installing a widget.

Two things we are deliberately not claiming. We have not tested every page against every assistive technology, so if you hit something that this statement says should work, we want to know rather than assume. And parts of the site that come from other companies, such as an embedded map, are not written by us and may not behave the way the rest of the site does.

Telling us something is broken

If something on this site does not work for you, ask us about it and we will fix what we can. Call the office on (916) 771-4151, or use the contact page. Tell us which page you were on and what happened, and if you can, what you were using to read it.

We have not named one person as the accessibility contact and we do not publish a separate address for these reports. The office phone line and the contact page both reach a person at the practice, and those are the routes to use today.

Ask us about an access issue, and we will tell you plainly what we can fix and what we cannot.
