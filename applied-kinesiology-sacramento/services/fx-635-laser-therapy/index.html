FX-635 Laser Therapy in Roseville

The FX-635 is a low-level laser, cleared by the FDA and used in this Roseville office as part of a treatment plan. It is non-thermal and hand-applied, and it is put on your case only when the whole-body assessment says the tissue is the right place to work.

Book an appointment Call the clinic

The point where the standard route runs out

Chronic heel, foot and soft-tissue pain usually gets the same sequence. Rest it. Anti-inflammatories. Orthotics or a night splint. A course of physical therapy. An imaging study that comes back looking more or less normal. Then a conversation about a steroid injection, shock wave, or eventually surgery.

That sequence is not wrong, and this practice does not tell anyone to walk away from their physician. What it does leave is a gap at the far end: once the tablets and the therapy have been used up, the next available step is usually invasive. A non-thermal laser is one of the few things that sits in that gap, and it costs you nothing in downtime to try it before you agree to a needle.

The device, described honestly

The FX-635 is an Erchonia low-level laser. Its first-party specifications are straightforward: 640 nm red light, FDA Class II, non-thermal, delivered through fixed heads over a treated area while you lie still. Because it does not heat tissue, there is no burn risk, no needle, no anaesthetic and no recovery time afterwards. You can walk out and go back to your day.

It carries FDA clearances K190572 and K180197. Plantar fasciitis is where this device matters most to this practice: it is the one condition locally where the practice owns a tool that only one other nearby clinic can offer at all. Every claim on this page is anchored to the manufacturer's own published specifications and clearance numbers. Link: plantar fasciitis.

What a course looks like

You are assessed before the laser is used at all. The applied-kinesiology examination and a physical exam come first, together with your history and whatever imaging you already have. Only then does a plan get built, and it is built from the whole stack: the adjustment, the FX-635, cold laser therapy, PEMF therapy, nutritional counselling, and hormone therapy management where it belongs.

Treatment runs as a course rather than a single visit, and you lie still while the heads are positioned over the area. Between sessions the same baseline tests get repeated so the plan answers to measured change. If the tissue is not responding, that is grounds to change course or refer you on, and you will be told so directly.

What the clearance numbers actually mean

FDA clearance means the device may be marketed for a stated use. That is a regulatory permission, not a finding that it will work for you. This practice states the clearance numbers, K190572 and K180197, and stops there. No trial statistic, success rate or pain-reduction percentage appears anywhere on this site.

A device is only as good as the decision to use it

Owning an FX-635 is not the differentiator. Deciding correctly whether you need one is.

Across the practices captured within a few miles of this office, the market splits cleanly in two. The clinics with the instruments, including laser, decompression and red light, publish no applied-kinesiology capability whatsoever. The regional practice with the deepest applied-kinesiology credentials publishes no laser, no PEMF and no device stack at all. This practice runs both halves under one licensed practitioner, which is why the laser here follows a finding rather than leading the sale. Links: how cold laser compares · the full treatment stack.
