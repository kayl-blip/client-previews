Hormone Therapy Management in Roseville

Some of what brings people here is not structural at all. Fatigue that sleep does not fix, recovery that has slowed down, symptoms that track with a hormone treatment already underway. This part of the practice is about managing the day-to-day side of that alongside your structural care, in coordination with the doctor who prescribes for you.

Book an appointment Call the clinic

Treated in pieces, by people who never speak to each other

The standard route handles hormones and handles pain, but it handles them separately. One clinician manages the prescription. Somebody else manages the back. A third person handles the physical therapy. Each appointment is short, each one covers its own piece, and nobody is looking at how the pieces interact.

The result is familiar: you are being treated for two or three things at once and you are the only person in the room who can see all of them. Nothing here replaces the prescribing clinician, and nothing here second-guesses a prescription. What it does is make sure the structural care, the diet and the recovery work are built around what is already happening, instead of ignoring it.

What "management" means here, precisely

Management here means the non-prescription side. Sleep, load, recovery, diet, how symptoms track across a week, and how all of that lines up with whatever your physician has you on. Prescribing, dosing and any change to a hormone treatment stay with the clinician who issued it. That boundary is not a technicality, it is the whole shape of this service.

What you get on this side of it is continuity. This is a single-practitioner practice: Dr. Brian Van Wagenen has been in practice since 1994, thirty-two years, and in Roseville since 2007. The same person takes the history, runs the assessment, does the structural work and reviews how the plan is holding up. Nothing is handed between staff and nothing has to be explained twice.

How it runs in practice

It starts where everything else here starts: a history, a physical examination, and the applied-kinesiology assessment. Bring your current prescriptions and any recent results your physician has shared with you, because the point of this appointment is to work with them rather than around them.

From there it is folded into the wider plan, which can draw on the chiropractic adjustment, FX-635 laser therapy, cold laser therapy, PEMF therapy and nutritional counselling. You are reassessed against the same baseline as you go. Anything that needs a prescribing decision goes back to your physician, in writing if that helps. Links: nutritional counselling · how the assessment works.

The boundary, stated plainly

This practice does not prescribe medication and does not administer hormone treatment. Nothing here diagnoses an endocrine condition, and no supplement or protocol on this site is offered as a treatment for one. The assessment supports standard diagnosis rather than replacing it, and FDA clearance of a device means cleared for a stated use, not proof of results.

Why this sits in the same building as the laser

Because a whole-body assessment that can only produce structural answers is not much of a whole-body assessment. If the examination keeps pointing at recovery, sleep and energy rather than at a joint, the practice needs somewhere for that to go.

That is the same logic behind the equipment. This is the only practice in the captured local set that runs a whole-body applied-kinesiology assessment and an FDA-cleared device stack together, under one licensed practitioner: the nearby instrumented clinics publish no applied-kinesiology capability, and the regionally strongest applied-kinesiology practice publishes no device stack. One assessment, one practitioner, and a plan that can reach structure, recovery and diet without three separate referrals. Link: the full treatment stack.

Specifics, not a credential contest

This practice will not win a certificate-list contest, and will not try. It offers a named assessment, named tools and a named next step.
