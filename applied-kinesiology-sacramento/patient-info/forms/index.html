Read this first

The forms are not downloadable here yet

The practice has always used paper intake forms and the previous site kept a forms page, but the files themselves are not published on this site. Rather than link you to something that is not there, this page tells you exactly what you will be asked for, so you can bring the information with you and fill the paperwork in once.

If you would rather have the forms in advance, ask for them when you book and the office will get them to you. What happens on a first visit covers the rest of the appointment.

What you will be asked for

Every California chiropractic office collects the same core set. Here is what each one is and why it exists.

  • Patient informationName, date of birth, address, phone and how to reach you. Bring a photo ID and, if you have one, your insurance card.
  • Health historyPast injuries, surgeries, current medication, and what has already been tried for the problem you are coming in with. The more of this you can write down beforehand, the more of the visit is spent on you rather than on a clipboard.
  • Description of the complaintWhere it hurts, when it started, what makes it worse and what makes it better. A rough timeline is more useful than a precise pain score.
  • Notice of Privacy Practices acknowledgementA signature confirming you were given the practice's HIPAA privacy notice. It records that you received it; it is not permission to share anything.
  • Consent to careWritten consent before any examination or treatment begins, after the plan has been explained to you. You can withdraw it at any point.
  • Good faith estimate requestIf you are uninsured or not using insurance, you have a federal right to a written estimate of expected costs before care is scheduled. Read how that works.
  • Progress reviewA short re-check of the original complaint at intervals during a course of care, so the plan is measured against what you came in for.
  • Records releaseWritten authorisation, signed by you, before any part of your record goes to another provider, an insurer or an attorney.

If you need something that is not here

Requests for records, forms for an employer or an insurer, and paperwork tied to a work injury or a collision claim are all handled case by case. Ask for what you need and the office will tell you what it can send and how long it takes.

Everything you write on a form is part of your clinical record. It is not used for marketing, it is not sold, and it does not leave the practice without your written authorisation. The privacy policy sets out how information is handled, and the practice's HIPAA notice governs the clinical record itself.

Nothing leaves without a signature.

Questions we get about paperwork

Three of them, more or less every week.

More about the appointment itself is on the patient information pages.

How early should I arrive?

Ten to fifteen minutes before the appointment is enough if you are filling the forms in at the office. If you have the information ready, it is quicker.

Can somebody fill these in for me?

Yes. A family member or carer can complete the forms with you, and the consent still has to be signed by the patient or by whoever holds legal authority to sign for them.

What if I am coming in about a car accident or a work injury?

Bring the claim number and the insurer or employer contact if you have them. Say so when you book so the right paperwork is ready. Ask us if you are not sure what applies, or book an appointment and sort it out at the desk.
