Your First Visit: What to Expect

Most people arrive having already tried something that did not hold. The first visit is built around finding out why.

It is an examination appointment before it is a treatment appointment. You will be asked a lot of questions, and you will be told what the answers mean.

Bring whatever you already have: imaging, lab work, a list of what has been tried. All of it shortens the guessing.

How applied kinesiology works

What happens, step by step

History, examination, findings, plan

Four things happen, in that order, and you are told what is being done at each stage.

  1. History. What the problem is, when it started, what makes it worse, what has already been tried and what happened when it was. This part runs longer than you are probably used to.
  2. Standard examination. Orthopedic and neurological screening, range of motion, and a look at posture and gait. The same screening any chiropractor should run.
  3. Whole-body assessment. Manual muscle testing across the region that hurts and well away from it, so the site of the pain can be separated from whatever is loading it.
  4. Findings. Said out loud, in plain language, including what they point to and what they do not.
  5. Plan. What is recommended, what it involves, and what it costs, discussed before anything is booked in.

Applied kinesiology assessment is used alongside standard diagnosis, not instead of it. If something needs a physician first, that is what you will be told.

Visit duration: the office has not published how long a first appointment runs. Ask when you book. This line gets replaced once the practice confirms it.

What to bring and how to prepare

Five minutes of preparation saves half the appointment

None of this is compulsory. All of it makes the examination faster and the findings better.

  • The patient forms, filled in before you arrive if you can.
  • Any imaging you have: X-ray, MRI or CT, or the report if you do not hold the images.
  • Recent lab work, especially if nutrition or fatigue is part of the picture.
  • A list of medication and supplements, with doses.
  • Clothes you can move and be tested in. Nothing that restricts a shoulder or a hip.

If you are still deciding whether this is the right appointment, the questions page answers most of what people call to ask.

If you are in a lot of pain

Say so when you book. The visit can be paced differently, and treatment on the day is a judgement call rather than a fixed part of the appointment.

What you will leave knowing

An explanation, and a plan with an end to it

The promise is narrow and specific. You leave with an explanation of what is driving the problem, and a plan you can hold this office to. Not an open-ended course of visits, and not a schedule you sign before anything has been examined.

This is the Roseville practice that combines applied kinesiology assessment with an FDA-cleared device stack, for pain that has not responded to anything else. If that combination is not what your problem needs, you hear it on the first visit rather than the tenth.

And if it is not working

A plan that is not producing what it should gets changed or ended. Nothing here is a guarantee, and no timeframe is promised.
