02 / What it is and what causes it

Not every headache comes from the same place.

A cervicogenic headache is a headache whose source is in the neck.

Joints, muscles and nerves in the upper neck refer pain up into the base of the skull, behind an eye, or across one side of the head. It often follows a neck injury, and it can feel very much like a migraine.

Migraine, tension-type headache and cluster headache have other drivers, including sleep, hormones, medication use and family history.

What we do not claim

Chiropractic care is used to treat headaches that originate in the neck. It does not treat every headache. If your neck is not the source, treating it is unlikely to change your headaches, and we would rather say that than start care.

04 / How we assess it

We look for the source, not just the symptom.

The exam is specific. Where the pain starts and where it travels. What sets it off. How your neck moves, and which segments are tender. Whether pressure on the upper neck reproduces the headache you actually get, rather than a general ache.

We take a full headache history, including any collision, fall or blow to the head, and when the headaches started relative to it.

Where soft tissue is involved we can perform diagnostic ultrasound on site, which images muscle, tendon and ligament without radiation.

If the picture points outside what we treat, that is a referral, not a treatment plan.

05 / How it is treated

Treating the neck rather than masking the headache.

When the neck is the source, care is directed at the neck.

That means chiropractic treatment of the segments involved, along with physical medicine: hands-on and equipment-based work on the muscle and joint tissue around them. It is non-surgical and drug-free.

We explain what a visit involves, and any risks it carries, before care begins. The aim is fewer headaches and less intense ones. It is not a promise that they stop, and any clinic that promises that is overreaching.

Individual results vary by person and by cause. We set a point to reassess, and if nothing has changed by then, the plan changes or we refer you on.

06 / When to seek care sooner

When a headache belongs in an emergency room.

Emergency signs

Call 911 or go to an emergency room for a headache that comes on suddenly and is the worst you have had, or one that arrives with a fever and a stiff neck, confusion, weakness on one side, slurred speech, loss of vision, or a seizure. The same applies to a headache after a blow to the head that keeps getting worse, or that comes with vomiting or drowsiness.

An emergency room is doing a different job than we are. It rules out what is life threatening and it treats the pain that day.

What it does not usually do is test for the soft-tissue and nerve damage that becomes a chronic headache weeks later. That part is ours.
