02 / What happens on the first visit

What the appointment involves

Five steps, in this order.

  1. Paperwork. You hand in the forms you completed at home, or fill them in at the desk.

  2. History. We ask how it happened, what hurts, and what you cannot do now that you could do before.

  3. Examination. Range of motion, orthopaedic testing and a neurological check.

  4. Testing on site, where the examination points to it. That can include electromyography, or EMG, which measures the electrical activity in a muscle; nerve conduction velocity, which measures how fast a signal travels along a nerve; a diagnostic vascular study; and digital X-ray. All of it is done here in one visit, without an outside referral.

  5. Findings. We go through what the testing measured and what we would do about it, and you leave with that in writing.

03 / What to bring

Bring these with you

If you were in a collision, read the list of what to bring before you come. A few documents change how completely your file can be recorded.

  • Photo identification and your insurance card.

  • Your completed intake, insurance and auto accident forms. They are on the patient forms page.

  • A list of everything you are taking, including anything prescribed since the collision.

  • Any imaging or paperwork you already have, including emergency room discharge notes and any X-ray or scan on disc.

If you were in a collision, also bring:

  • The police report, or the exchange of information slip if no report was filed.

  • Your claim number and the name of the adjuster handling it, if a claim is open.

  • Your attorney's name and contact details, if you have engaged one.

Missing something

If something on this list is missing, come anyway and send it on later. None of it stops us examining you.

04 / What it costs

The evaluation costs you nothing

The first consultation and evaluation is $0 out of pocket. What happens after that depends on your case and on your coverage, so we check yours before treatment starts rather than after.

If you were injured in a collision, care can often proceed on a lien, which means nothing is paid by you while your claim is open, if you qualify. A lien is a written agreement to be paid out of the claim later, and it carries real conditions, including what happens if the claim does not settle.

We set all of that out on the medical liens and billing page. We do not advise you on the claim itself. That is your attorney's work, not ours.
