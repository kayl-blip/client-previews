02 / Why measurement beats description

Description is disputable. A measurement is harder to wave away.

03 / Channel 01, electromyography

Electromyography (EMG)

EMG records the electrical signal a muscle produces at rest and while you contract it. A muscle whose nerve supply has been disturbed fires differently from a healthy one, and the recording shows that difference as a pattern rather than an opinion.

An abnormal result points to which nerve or muscle is actually involved, so care can target that structure instead of treating the whole neck the same way. A normal result is worth having too. It moves nerve involvement down the list and sends us back to the soft tissue. Findings and results vary from person to person.

RecordedAt rest and while you contract

04 / Channel 02, nerve conduction

Nerve conduction velocity testing

This test measures how fast an electrical signal travels along a nerve. A small pulse is delivered at one point on the limb and picked up at another, and the equipment times the trip. Where the signal slows, that is where the problem is.

It is the test that diagnoses carpal tunnel syndrome, and it is the test that matters when symptoms radiate: pain, numbness, tingling or weakness that runs down an arm or a leg instead of staying in the neck or the back. Radiating symptoms are common after a collision, and they are often put down to muscle strain without ever being measured. Results vary by person and by cause.

  • 2pointsA pulse delivered at one point on the limb, picked up at another
  • m/srecordedThe equipment times the trip, so the result is a speed and not a score

05 / Channel 03, blood flow

Diagnostic vascular studies

These studies look at blood flow rather than nerve signal. Sound waves are used to watch blood move through an artery or a vein and to show where flow is restricted. Nothing is injected and nothing breaks the skin.

After trauma the study is indicated when a limb is cold, discolored, swollen, or numb in a pattern that does not follow a nerve, and when the force of the collision crossed a major vessel. It also works as a rule-out: a normal study removes a possibility and narrows what we are treating. Findings vary case by case.

Indicated after trauma when

  • A limb is cold
  • Discolored
  • Swollen
  • Numb off a nerve pattern
  • Force crossed a major vessel

06 / Ultrasound and digital X-ray

The baseline layer

Diagnostic ultrasound images soft tissue: muscle, tendon and ligament, the structures that take the load in a whiplash injury and the ones an X-ray cannot show. High-frequency digital X-ray shows bone, alignment and joint spacing, and it is what we look at first when there is any reason to rule out a fracture.

The honest note about X-ray is that it is ordinary. Digital X-ray is standard equipment across this market, and four of the six competing practices measured 2026-08-20 also offer diagnostic ultrasound. Neither is our differentiator. They are the layer underneath the nerve and vascular testing, not a substitute for it.

  2. Digital X-rayBone, alignment and joint spacing
