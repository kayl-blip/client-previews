01 / Non-surgical decompression, San Jose

Non-surgical spinal decompression in San Jose

Disc pain does not have to begin with an operation. Non-surgical spinal decompression is a treatment that slowly unloads one segment of your spine, in the neck or in the lower back. Whether it is the right treatment for you depends on what your examination and imaging show, so that is where we start.

02 / Disc problems, and where they travel

Who it is for

This treatment is aimed at disc problems: bulging, herniated and inflamed intervertebral discs. A disc is the cushion between two bones of the spine. When it is injured it can press on the nerve root beside it, which is why disc pain often does not stay in your back. It travels. Into a buttock and down a leg, or across a shoulder and into an arm, and sometimes as numbness, tingling or weakness rather than as pain. If sitting, bending or coughing makes it worse, tell us at the evaluation. Decompression is also for patients who want to try a non-surgical path before considering surgery. It does not suit every case, and what it cannot do is set out further down this page.

Where it turns up

  • Into a buttock and down a leg
  • Across a shoulder and into an arm
  • Numbness
  • Tingling
  • Weakness
  • Worse sitting, bending or coughing

03 / The table and the pull

How it works

You stay clothed and lie on a treatment table. A harness holds you, and the table applies a slow, controlled pull along the line of your spine. That pull lowers the pressure inside the disc. Less pressure means more room for an irritated nerve root and a better environment for an inflamed disc to settle. The pull is applied and released in cycles rather than held steady, so the muscles around your spine are less likely to guard against it. We treat both the neck, which is the cervical spine, and the lower back, which is the lumbar spine. Individual results vary, and nobody can tell you in advance how your disc will respond.

04 / Sequence, frequency, review points

What a course of care looks like

Your first visit is an examination, not a treatment. We take your history, examine you, run the testing that is indicated, and then explain what we found and what we propose before anything begins.

After that, decompression runs as a course rather than a single session, at a set frequency, with review points built into the plan from the start. At each review we re-examine you and compare the findings against your starting measurements. If nothing is moving, we change the plan or refer you on rather than book you in for more of the same.

  • 1stvisitAn examination, not a treatment
  • SetfrequencyA course, not a single session
  • ReviewpointsBuilt into the plan from the start

05 / Imaging and nerve testing, on site

How we confirm it is the right treatment

We image before we decompress. High-frequency digital X-ray is performed on site and shows the bones: alignment, the space between vertebrae, fracture, and degenerative change. It does not show the disc itself, which is why an X-ray alone is never the whole answer. Where your symptoms point to a nerve being involved, we can measure that here too, in the same visit, with nerve conduction velocity testing and electromyography (EMG). Those tests record how fast a nerve is conducting a signal and how the muscle it supplies is behaving. The reasoning is simple. We would rather know which nerve is affected than treat the level that looks most likely.

  1. Digital X-rayBone, alignment, joint spacing, fracture and wear
  2. Conduction velocityThe speed of a signal travelling along a nerve
  3. ElectromyographyThe behaviour of the muscle that nerve supplies

06 / Scope limits, stated

What decompression does not do

Screened before we start

Never after

It is not a permanent fix, and we will not describe it as one. It does not repair every damaged disc, and it is not a substitute for surgery in the cases that genuinely need surgery. Some findings rule it out entirely, and we screen for those before we start rather than after. Where your examination or imaging points to something outside the scope of this treatment, we refer you for MRI or other imaging immediately, and we tell you that on the day rather than running a course first. If your symptoms are severe or getting worse quickly, seek urgent medical care first. If we do not think decompression will help you, we will say so at the evaluation.

What it is not

  • A permanent fix
  • A repair for every disc
  • A substitute for surgery
  • Urgent care

07 / Where it applies

Conditions this treatment addresses

Low back pain is the most common reason patients ask about decompression, particularly when the pain is disc-related and has not settled with rest. Neck pain follows the same logic when a cervical disc is involved. Pain that radiates out of the spine and into a limb belongs here too, because that pattern usually points at the nerve root rather than the muscle. Results vary by person and by cause.

08 / Before you book

Common questions

How soon can I be seen?

Same-day appointments are usually available [UNCONFIRMED]. Call the clinic at (408) 292-2225 [UNCONFIRMED] and ask for the next opening.

Does it hurt?

Treatment is non-surgical and drug-free. We explain the risks before care begins, and if something hurts during a session, say so and we stop and reassess.

How many sessions will I need?

That depends on your findings. You get a planned number at the evaluation rather than an open-ended commitment, and individual results vary.

Will I still end up needing surgery?

We cannot promise you either way. Some patients respond to non-surgical care and some do not. If your findings point toward a surgical opinion, we say so and refer you for one.

What does it cost?

The consultation and evaluation is offered at no cost to you. What treatment itself costs, and whether any of it is billed to insurance or handled on a lien, depends on your policy and your case. We check yours and tell you before you start.
