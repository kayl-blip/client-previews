02 / What it is and what causes it

Sciatica is a symptom, and it has several possible causes.

Sciatica is irritation of the sciatic nerve, which runs from the lower back through the buttock and down the leg. The pain can burn, shoot or feel electric, and it often comes with numbness, pins and needles, or weakness in the leg or foot. The irritation can begin in more than one place.

Four possible origins

DiscA bulging or herniated disc can press on a nerve root where it leaves the spine.

Bony channelThe bony channel the nerve passes through can narrow.

MuscleA muscle in the buttock can compress the nerve.

CollisionA collision can injure any of those structures at once.

Why it matters

They are different problems, they need different care, and from the outside they look alike.

03 / How we assess it

We locate the nerve involvement instead of inferring it.

Two tests do most of the work.

Nerve conduction

Nerve conduction velocity testing measures how fast and how well a signal travels along a nerve.

Electromyography

Electromyography, or EMG, records the electrical activity in the muscles that nerve supplies.

Together they help identify which nerve level is actually involved, rather than working backward from where you feel the pain. Both are performed here, in one visit, without a referral out. High-frequency digital X-ray is on site as well, and it is worth being clear about what it does: it shows bone, not nerve function.

04 / How it is treated

Care aimed at the source of the irritation.

The findings decide the plan.

Two non-surgical options

Spinal decompressionWhere a disc is involved, non-surgical spinal decompression is one option for the lower back: a table-based traction treatment that takes pressure off the disc.

Chiropractic careChiropractic care addresses the joints and the soft tissue around the nerve.

Nothing here is surgical and nothing is a prescription. We explain what is involved, and any risks it carries, before care starts.

Leg pain settles quickly for one person and slowly for another. Individual results vary by person and by cause. We reassess at set points, and if your symptoms are not moving, the plan changes rather than repeats.

05 / When to seek care sooner

Some leg pain needs a hospital, not an appointment.

Emergency signs

Go to an emergency room now, or call 911, if you lose control of your bladder or bowel, if you have numbness through the groin or inner thighs, if both legs are weak, if a foot starts to drag, or if weakness is getting worse by the hour. Back and leg pain with a fever belongs there too.

An emergency room is doing a different job than we are: it rules out what is life threatening and it treats the pain in front of it. When our exam points to something we cannot image here, we refer you for MRI or other imaging straight away, and we tell you what we are looking for.
