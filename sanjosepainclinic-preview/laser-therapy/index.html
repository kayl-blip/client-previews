01 / Laser therapy, San Jose

Laser therapy in San Jose, as one part of a treatment plan

Cold and hot laser therapy are non-surgical, drug-free options we use to support soft-tissue recovery alongside hands-on care. We examine you first and use laser only where the findings support it. Results vary by person and by injury.

02 / Who it is for

Who laser therapy is for

Laser therapy is generally considered for soft-tissue problems: strains, sprains, tendon and muscle injuries, and inflammation that is slow to settle. Sports injuries are a common reason patients ask about it, particularly a strain that keeps coming back when training resumes. It is also used alongside treatment for neck, shoulder and low back pain where the soft tissue is still irritated. If you were hurt in a collision, laser may form part of a wider plan, but it is never the whole plan. Whether it suits you depends on the examination, not on the request. Individual results vary.

Considered for

  • Strains
  • Sprains
  • Tendon and muscle injuries
  • Inflammation slow to settle
  • Sports injuries

03 / The treatment itself

What the treatment involves

Cold laser therapy, also called low-level laser therapy, applies a low-power light to the skin over the injured area. The handpiece rests on or just above the skin, and sessions are short. Hot laser uses a higher power setting, and you feel warmth in the tissue. Neither is a procedure. Nothing is cut, injected or prescribed. We use laser as an adjunct, which means it sits alongside adjustment, rehabilitation exercise and the rest of your plan rather than replacing any of it. On its own it does not correct the mechanical problem that produced the injury, which is why it is never booked here as a stand-alone course of care.

04 / A course of care

How a course of treatment runs

  1. Care starts with an examination, not with a session. If laser fits the findings, we set a short block of visits and a date to review them.

  2. Sessions are usually combined with the rest of your visit rather than booked on their own.

  3. At the review we assess again and compare against the baseline we took at the start. If nothing has changed, we say so, and we change the plan or refer you on.

Continuing a treatment that is not working is not a plan.

Results vary by person and by injury, and the number of sessions varies with them.

05 / Deciding first

How we decide, before we treat

We examine and test before we treat. High-frequency digital X-ray is on site, so if we need to see bone alignment or rule out a fracture, that happens in the same visit. X-ray is useful, but it images bone. It will not show you a nerve that has stopped conducting properly. Where your symptoms point at nerve involvement, we test nerve function directly on site: electromyography, which measures the electrical activity in a muscle, and nerve conduction velocity, which measures how fast a signal travels along a nerve. The findings decide the treatment, including whether laser has any part in it.

  1. Digital X-rayBone alignment, and ruling out a fracture
  2. EMG and conduction velocityNerve function, measured rather than assumed

06 / Scope limits

What laser therapy does not do

Out of scope

Screened before session one

Laser therapy does not repair a fracture, reduce a disc herniation, or replace surgical assessment where surgery is the right answer. It does not cure any condition, and we will not describe it that way. It is not emergency care. If you have been in a collision and have head, chest or abdominal symptoms, an emergency department is the right place first, because ruling out a life-threatening injury is a different job from treating soft tissue. Hot laser adds heat to the tissue, so we hold it back where heat is not appropriate and use the cold setting or another form of treatment instead. There are also situations where we will not use laser at all, and we screen for those before the first session.

  1. repair a fracture
  2. reduce a disc herniation
  3. replace surgical assessment
  4. cure any condition
  5. stand in for emergency care

07 / Where it fits

Conditions we use it alongside

Laser therapy is one option inside care for several of the conditions we treat. It is also used where stress-related physical tension keeps the muscles of the neck and shoulders loaded and sore. You will see a figure quoted in this market that stress accounts for 80-90% of disease. We do not repeat that figure, because we have no source for it. We treat the physical tension in front of us, not the stress behind it. Each condition page sets out how we assess that problem and which treatments apply. Individual results vary.

Neck and shoulder pain, including pain that began after a collision.

Low back pain, including disc-related pain.

Sciatica and radiating leg pain, where we test the nerve rather than assume it.

Headaches that begin in the neck.

All conditions we treat.

09 / Asked often

Common questions

Does it hurt?

Most people feel nothing from the cold laser beyond mild warmth, and the hot laser feels warm. Tell us straight away if you feel more than that, and we stop.

How soon can I be seen?

Same-day appointments are usually available. [UNCONFIRMED] Call and we will tell you what is open today.

How many sessions will I need?

That depends on the injury and on what the review shows. We set the review point at the start so the plan has an end you can see, and outcomes differ from person to person.

Will my insurance cover it?

That depends on your policy, and after a collision it also depends on your claim. We check your coverage and tell you what we find before treatment starts. We cannot promise that any insurer will pay.

Can I have laser on the same visit as my adjustment?

Usually yes.
