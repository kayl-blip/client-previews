01 / Medical liens and billing

Medical liens, explained in plain language

A medical lien is how you can be treated after a car accident without paying at the time. It is also a contract, and you are entitled to understand its terms before you agree to it. This page sets out who bills, who is responsible if the claim does not settle, and what happens to the balance at the end.

02 / What a lien actually is

The mechanism, not the slogan

Lien or letter of protection

Order of events

01020304

A medical lien is a written agreement that your care is paid for out of what your claim produces, instead of out of your pocket while the claim is open. A letter of protection does a similar job, and is issued by your attorney's office rather than by you. The sequence is usually the same in either case. You are examined, care begins, the clinic bills against the case, and the account is settled when the case is. The specifics matter more than the summary, and we will not publish specifics we have not verified.

  • 01You are examined
  • 02Care begins
  • 03The clinic bills against the case
  • 04The account is settled when the case is

03 / Who bills, and when

Where the bill goes

For most auto accident care, the bill goes to the at-fault driver's insurer, through your claim. Your own policy may also be involved, depending on the coverage you carry. You may have read somewhere that your auto insurance will pay 100% of your care. We will not tell you that. Coverage depends on your policy, the other driver's policy and the facts of the collision, and none of that can be known before it has been read. We check your coverage before treatment starts and we tell you what we find, including when the honest answer is that it is still uncertain. If a claim number has already been opened, bring it to your first visit.

  1. Primary routeThe at-fault driver's insurer, through your claim
  2. Also possibleYour own policy, depending on the coverage you carry
  3. Bring it inThe claim number, where one is already open

04 / What happens if the claim does not settle

The question nobody answers, answered as far as we can

Who carries it

If your claim fails, or settles for less than the cost of your treatment, the balance does not disappear. Someone is responsible for it under the terms of whatever was signed, and who that is depends on the lien document itself.

05 / If you have no attorney yet

Get examined first

You do not need an attorney to be examined here. In most cases the examination should come first anyway. Soft-tissue and nerve findings recorded close to the date of the collision give a baseline, and later testing can be compared against it to show what changed. Get looked at, get the findings documented, then make your decision about representation with the documentation already in hand. We do not give legal advice. We do not tell you whether to hire anyone, and we do not require you to use any particular firm. If you already have an attorney, tell us at intake so records go to the right office. What we send to an attorney's office is described on our attorney referrals page.

06 / If you are paying yourself

Self-pay, and what it costs

Not every injury has a claim behind it. If you are paying for care yourself, ask for the numbers before you commit to anything.

  • No price list

    We do not publish service prices on this site, because the work-up one person needs is not the work-up the next person needs, and a price list would mislead more than it would inform.

  • Instead

    What you get instead is a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges, in writing, before non-emergency care begins. The initial consultation and evaluation is offered at no out-of-pocket cost to you. Individual results vary, and so does the length of a course of care.

07 / Your right to a Good Faith Estimate

A written estimate, before care begins

If you are uninsured, or insured but not using your insurance for this care, federal law gives you the right to a Good Faith Estimate of what your treatment is expected to cost, in writing, before non-emergency care begins. This comes from the No Surprises Act. Ask for it at any point, or we will offer it. If your final bill arrives substantially above the estimate, the law provides a dispute route. Our full notice, including how to request an estimate and how the dispute process works, is on the Good Faith Estimate page.

  1. Who it coversUninsured, or insured and not using that insurance here
  2. If the bill runs overA dispute route provided by federal law

08 / Common questions

Billing questions we are actually asked

Q. Will I be asked for money at the first visit?

A. You will be told the answer when you book, not when you arrive.

Q. How long does the first visit take, and what should I bring?

A. Bring photo ID, your insurance cards, the police report or the exchange-of-information slip if you have one, and the claim number if a claim has been opened.

Q. Will you bill my health insurance as well?

A. Sometimes that is the right route and sometimes it is not, depending on your policy and on whether an auto claim is open. We check before anything is billed.
